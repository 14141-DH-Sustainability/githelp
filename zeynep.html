Contested Objects: Provenance and Restitution in Berlin's Museums

Berlin's museums hold some of the most famous objects in the world, from the Ishtar Gate and the Pergamon Altar to the bust of Nefertiti and the Benin Bronzes. Many of them reached Berlin through excavations under unequal agreements, diplomatic gifts between empires, colonial expeditions or looting, and several are the subject of ongoing restitution claims.

This project will build a small digital exhibition of ten such objects. Following the idea of the "cultural biography of objects" (Kopytoff 1986; Gosden and Marshall 1999), each object will be presented through its life history: where it was made and what it meant there, how it came to Berlin, what happened to it since, and who wants it back today.

The data will be kept in two linked tables, one for the objects and one for the stations of each object's life, with dates, places and sources. A Google Sheet will feed the exhibition pages, and an interactive map will trace every object's journey from its place of origin to Berlin and, in some cases, back again. All images will be openly licensed.

The site will be static and hosted on GitHub Pages, with its data stored in open formats, so that it can stay online and be reused with minimal maintenance.
