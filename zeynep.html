From Signature to Withdrawal: An Online Archive of the Istanbul Convention in Turkey (2011–2021)

The Council of Europe Convention on preventing and combating violence against women and domestic violence was opened for signature in Istanbul in 2011, and Turkey was the first country to ratify it. Ten years later, in March 2021, Turkey withdrew from the very convention that carries the name of its largest city, through a presidential decree that took effect in July 2021.

This project will build a small, sustainable online archive that documents this decade. It will gather the key events, actors, legal documents, public campaigns and protests surrounding the Convention into one structured, browsable collection. Each entry will include a date, a short description, a place, an image where possible, and references to its sources.

The archive will also contain an interactive map of the protests against the withdrawal across Turkish cities, and a short section on how the Convention came to be framed in public debate as a threat to the family and to national values.

Much of the material on this topic exists in news articles, social media posts and NGO websites that may disappear over time. By collecting it in a static, freely hosted and openly licensed website, the project aims to keep this record accessible for researchers and the public in the long term.
